1. The product in one page

Aj Smart Biz gives a small or mid-sized business a complete online presence and a back office in one subscription: a branded public website on its own domain, a product and service catalogue, a cart and order desk, appointment booking, stock control, counter invoicing, customer accounts, enquiry inboxes and visitor analytics. Every business (a tenant or company) runs on one shared platform. The platform operator sells plans, provisions companies and controls which features each plan includes.

52

The single idea behind the platform One deployment per website design serves every company that uses it. The website reads the domain it was opened on, asks the API which company owns that domain, and brands itself from the answer. Nothing is built per tenant, so onboarding a new business is a database operation and takes minutes, not a project.

2. Problem and opportunity

The problem

  • Small businesses (salons, decor and framing studios, retailers, service providers) need a website, a way to take orders and bookings, and simple stock and billing — but buy each from a different vendor, or not at all.
  • Agency-built websites are expensive, go stale, and cannot take an order or a booking.
  • Generic site builders do not know about branches, GST invoices, WhatsApp-first ordering, or Indian payment habits (UPI, cash on delivery).

The opportunity

  • One subscription that bundles website + catalogue + orders + bookings + stock + billing + leads, sized by plan.
  • WhatsApp-first: orders and enquiries can be handed to the business's WhatsApp, which is how its customers already talk to it.
  • Recurring revenue (monthly to yearly plans), with upsell built in: tenants browse higher plans and request an upgrade from inside their console.

5. Business model

Plans and subscriptions

Revenue comes from plans sold to companies as dated subscriptions. A plan defines:

  • Price (with optional discount price), currency and billing cycle — monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, yearly or lifetime — plus optional trial days.
  • Quotas — maximum branches, maximum admins, included users and storage (MB).
  • Functionalities — which sellable features the plan grants (see section 6).
  • Visibility — shown on the public pricing page (isPublic), offered to tenants for self-requested upgrades (showInAdmin), marked “popular”.

Actual price points are configured by the platform owner in Plan Management and are not fixed in code.

Revenue levers built into the product

Upgrade requests

Tenants see every requestable plan labelled Upgrade or Downgrade and ask for it in one click. The request lands at the top of the platform's renewal desk.

Fair proration

Mid-term changes credit unused days by daily value: credit = term total × remaining days ÷ term days, so monthly and yearly plans compare fairly.

Pre-renewal & auto-renew

The next term can be booked before the current one ends; auto-renew rolls terms over automatically.

Feature-based tiers

Website sections, cart, stock, invoices and customer accounts are separate grants, so plans can be tiered by value delivered.

What happens when a plan lapses

Expired, suspended or missing plans block creating branches and admins, hide paid menus, and replace the public website with a polite holding page (still branded, noindex, never mentioning price). This gives a strong, fair reason to renew without destroying the tenant's data — nothing is ever deleted.

7. Design principles that run through the product

  1. Nothing is deleted. Almost every table uses soft delete; deleting a company soft-deletes its branches, roles and admins together, and restore brings them all back.
  2. History is frozen. A subscription snapshots the plan; an order line snapshots the product and price; an order copies the address as text. Editing the source later never rewrites the past.
  3. One answer, many screens. Entitlement, quota, offer status and enquiry routing are each decided by one function, and every screen renders that answer — so a disabled button and a refused request never disagree.
  4. Absent, not empty. A website section with no content or no entitlement is not rendered at all — no empty boxes, no invented copy.
  5. Prices come from the server. The public cart sends product ids and quantities only; every price is taken from the catalogue at order time.
  6. API-first. All content the websites show comes from the API; templates hold no business data.

8. Current state and next steps

Built and verified

  • All five applications build cleanly (dev and production).
  • 188 end-to-end API checks and 63 browser checks recorded in the project README.
  • Docker images and CI/CD to a self-hosted server with nginx and TLS.

Known gaps (see Document 11)

  • No SMS gateway yet — OTP uses a development code.
  • No online payment gateway; payment is UPI/link hand-off.
  • No automated test suite in the repositories.
  • maxUsers and some quotas are displayed but not metered.
  • Refresh tokens are issued but not used by the consoles.
