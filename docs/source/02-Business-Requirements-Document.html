1. Business objectives

#ObjectiveHow the platform meets it

3. Scope

In scope

  • Tenant provisioning, billing records, plan lifecycle
  • Role-based access for company staff
  • Public tenant websites in three designs
  • Catalogue, cart, order desk, stock, POS invoicing
  • Services, enquiries and slot booking
  • Customer accounts (phone + OTP)
  • Visitor tracking, leads, analytics
  • Product marketing website and its CMS

Out of scope (today)

  • Online payment gateway capture (UPI/link hand-off only)
  • SMS delivery of OTPs (development code until a gateway is connected)
  • Email notifications
  • Accounting ledgers / GST return filing
  • Native mobile apps
  • Per-tenant custom code or builds

5. Business rules catalogue

RuleStatement
R-01 Entitlementactive = granted (plan snapshot) AND enabled (tenant switch) AND served (plan not expired/suspended). Decided only in functionality.service.js.
R-02 Subscription statespending → active → expired/cancelled/superseded; active ↔ suspended; pending → cancelled. superseded only via plan change. All other moves refused.
R-03 Prorationcredit = term total × remaining days ÷ term days; previewed before commit.
R-04 Quota refusal reasonslimit_reached, expired, suspended, no_plan; plan-wide reasons outrank per-metric ones.
R-05 Limits sourceLimits and grants read from the subscription snapshot, never the live plan row.
R-06 One open requestAt most one pending plan request per company.
R-07 Order lifecyclepending → confirmed → packed → dispatched → delivered; any non-terminal → cancelled. Never backward. No manual order creation, no deletion; lines are read-only.
R-08 Unpriced linesLines priced in words are counted in unpricedItems; totals are never treated as complete when such lines exist.
R-09 Stock directionDirection comes from the reason (purchase in, damage out, count = adjust). sale and transfer are system-written only.
R-10 Available stockavailable = quantity − reserved. Tracked products derive in/out of stock from the shelf.
R-11 OfferDiscount % is computed from price and offer price; an offer price ≥ price is refused. isOnOffer is the single decision.
R-12 Category treeMaximum three levels; no move under its own descendant; deleting promotes children and un-categorises products.
R-13 Enquiry routingTarget is both (default), admin only, or WhatsApp only; without an inquiry number WhatsApp degrades to admin.
R-14 WhatsApp numbersTyped inquiry/contact/support/orders; empty type falls back to contact; country prefixes normalised.
R-15 Customer identityPhone is the identity, unique per company; normalised; not editable by staff; barred customers keep their orders.
R-16 OTP6 digits, 10-minute validity, 5 attempts; development code until an SMS gateway exists.
R-17 Address on orderCopied as text at order time, never referenced.
R-18 Branch fallbackBranch content overrides company content; a branch with none inherits; a company domain never shows branch content.
R-19 Soft deleteEverything is paranoid except switch rows, ledgers, stock levels and visit logs.
R-20 Invoicesdraft → issued → cancelled; only drafts are edited or deleted; issuing deducts stock, cancelling returns it.

6. Key business processes

6.1 Onboard a new business

  1. Prospect books a demo on the product website → enquiry appears in Our website → Enquiries.
  2. Operator creates the plan (if needed) in Plan Management.
  3. Operator creates the company: profile, branding, main admin, plan and payment. Optionally duplicates a template company or runs the AI content creator.
  4. Operator adds the tenant's domain; DevOps adds the nginx vhost pointing to the chosen design.
  5. Main admin signs in at the company workspace, sets a new password, switches on features and fills content.
  6. The website goes live as soon as the domain resolves.

6.2 Renew or change plan

  1. Tenant sees days remaining on My Plan; operator sees “Expiring in 7/30 days” on Company Plans.
  2. Operator pre-renews (queued as pending) or tenant raises a plan request.
  3. Operator previews proration, approves; the old term becomes superseded, a transaction is recorded.
  4. The sweeper activates queued terms on their start date.

6.3 Order to delivery

  1. Visitor adds items to the cart and submits name, phone and address (or picks a saved address).
  2. API prices the order from the catalogue, records it as pending, and returns the WhatsApp message or payment link.
  3. Staff confirm (stock reserved), pack, dispatch (stock issued, ledger entry), deliver. Payment is marked separately.
  4. Cancelling returns or releases stock. Analytics exclude cancelled orders from revenue.

6.4 Service booking

  1. Visitor opens a bookable service, picks a date; available slots are computed from hours, slot length, capacity and existing bookings.
  2. Booking is recorded as a service lead (requested). Staff accept or decline; completed bookings feed revenue.

6.5 Counter sale (POS)

  1. Staff open a new invoice, pick products, discounts and tax; save as draft or issue.
  2. Issuing assigns the invoice number and deducts stock from the selected warehouse; payments may be partial.

7. Non-functional requirements

AreaRequirement
SecurityJWT with scope; principal reloaded on every request; bcrypt passwords; helmet; rate limits on login and public writes; uploads served with a script-blocking CSP.
IsolationTenant derived from token, never from client input; unknown domains return platform defaults (no enumeration).
AuditabilitySubscription events, stock ledger, created/updated by, soft delete.
PerformanceStock level table avoids summing the ledger; catalogue stripped from client payloads; product site caches API data 300 s with snapshot fallback.
AvailabilityDocker health checks, automatic rollback on failed deploy, “server unreachable” screens in every client.
UsabilityEvery refusal carries a human-readable reason that the UI shows verbatim.
SEOServer-rendered websites, sitemap/robots on the product site, noindex on holding pages.
LocalisationCurrency and timezone per company; Indian states and GST codes seeded.

8. Assumptions, constraints and dependencies

  • Database: MySQL (default) — SQLite supported for local work. Schema is created by Sequelize sync; there are no migration files.
  • Hosting: one Linux server running Docker containers behind nginx, with Cloudflare DNS and certbot TLS.
  • Messaging: WhatsApp is reached through wa.me links; no WhatsApp Business API integration.
  • AI: the AI content creator depends on an Anthropic API key configured for the backend.
  • Payments: recorded manually by the operator (platform billing) or handed off to UPI/payment links (tenant orders).
